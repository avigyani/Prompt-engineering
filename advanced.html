Module 1 · Lesson 1

The RTCF Framework — Role, Task, Context, Format

Most prompts fail for the same reason: they ask the AI to guess. "Write about marketing" leaves everything open — the audience, the length, the tone, the purpose. The AI guesses, and the answer feels generic. The fix is a structure used by professional prompt engineers: RTCF.

Role tells the AI who to be: "Act as a senior copywriter." Task says exactly what to do: "Write a 150-word product description." Context gives the facts it needs: the product, the audience, the price. Format says how the answer should look: "Three short paragraphs, no bullet points, end with a question." Four parts, one sentence each, and the same request produces a completely different answer.

The framework works because each part removes one guess. Role sets the standard. Task sets the goal. Context sets the facts. Format sets the shape. When an answer is weak, one of the four is usually missing — add it, and the next answer improves immediately.

Before and after

Weak: "Write about my restaurant."
Strong: "Act as a food writer for a Bengaluru audience. Write a 120-word Instagram caption for a seafood restaurant in Chikkabanavara, announcing a weekend family thali at ₹799. Mention the sea-facing seating and the live grill counter. Tone: warm and inviting, no hype words. End with a call to action: book a table this weekend."

Practice task: take one prompt you used this week and rewrite it with all four RTCF parts. Run both versions and compare the answers side by side.
